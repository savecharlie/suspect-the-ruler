The note a moving ear hears as a chord

What a Lorentz boost really does to a decaying thing — and why the width of the answer is the speed of light.

Everyone who has met relativity knows the first trick. A moving clock runs slow. A muon born high in the atmosphere, racing down at nearly light speed, lives long enough to reach the ground because its little internal clock is dilated by the Lorentz factor γ. Motion stretches time. So here is a question that sounds like it has an obvious answer: take something that decays — a hot ember cooling, a plucked string ringing down, a disturbed fluid settling back to rest — and watch it go past you at high speed. How does its decay look?

The obvious answer is: slower. By γ. Same as the muon. The thing relaxes at some rate 1/τ in its own frame; you, watching it fly by, should see it relax at rate 1/(γτ). Time dilation, applied to a decay. It feels airtight.

It is wrong, and the way it is wrong is beautiful.

One number, or a smear of numbers

The airtight feeling comes from a hidden assumption: that the decay is one thing with one rate. A single relaxation mode. And a single rate really is a single number — write the settling as e^{−t/τ} and its whole character is the one pole at frequency ω = −i/τ, a single point in the plane of complex frequencies. If a boost only dilated time, it would just slide that point closer to the origin. One number in, one number out.

In January 2026 Lorenzo Gavassino worked out what a Lorentz boost actually does to such a spectrum, and published it that summer (Phys. Rev. Lett. 137, 022301). The headline: a boost does not slide the point. It shatters it into a continuum — one relaxation rate becomes a whole band of them. One note becomes a chord. And the culprit is not time dilation at all. It is the other half of relativity, the half we always forget: the relativity of simultaneity.

Why “now” tilts, and why that matters

Here is the mechanism, in a form you can hold. Any real relaxation is carried by many small carriers — in a gas, literal particles; in a field theory, the modes — and they stream in every direction at their own speeds. When we say the disturbance is spatially uniform in the rest frame — the same everywhere, no ripples, wavenumber k = 0 — we are saying that at one instant, every carrier is caught doing the same thing. They are all in phase. Their contributions add up coherently into one clean note at the one rate 1/τ.

But “at one instant” is exactly the phrase relativity refuses to let two observers share. The moving observer’s now is a tilted slice through spacetime. On that tilt, the carriers are no longer caught in phase: the ones streaming forward are sampled a little later in their motion, the ones streaming backward a little earlier. What looked uniform on the flat slice has ripples on the tilted one. “Uniform now,” boosted, secretly contains spatial structure — a nonzero wavenumber, k ≠ 0.

And spatial structure wakes the streaming. A carrier moving at velocity v through a ripple of wavenumber k beats against it at frequency ω = v·k. Each carrier now sings a different pitch, set by its own velocity. The single pitch fans out into a band of pitches — one for every velocity in the ensemble. That is the continuum. The moving ear hears the chord because its “now” tilted the carriers out of unison.

You can watch the whole thing collapse to a single formula. For the simplest kinetic model — carriers spread uniformly over velocities up to the maximum signal speed — the settling seen at wavenumber k is exactly

$$C(t) = e^{-t/\tau}\,\operatorname{sinc}(v_{\max}\,k\,t), \qquad \operatorname{sinc}(x)=\frac{\sin x}{x}.$$

At rest, k = 0, the sinc is 1 and you get the clean single mode e^{−t/τ}. Its spectrum is one sharp peak. Boosted, k is forced away from zero, and the spectrum becomes that peak convolved with a boxcar — a flat band. A spike, smeared into a plateau.

The width is the speed of light

Now the part that stopped me. That band is not arbitrarily wide. The fastest any carrier can stream is v_max — the light cone, the hard ceiling that made these theories causal in the first place. So the band’s edge sits exactly at

$$\omega = \pm\, v_{\max}\, k.$$

The width of the chord is the maximum signal speed. Causality doesn’t merely bound the spreading — it is the spreading. The same constant that forbids information from outrunning light is the constant that sets how far a boost fans a note into a chord. I built the spectrum numerically and measured its full width at half maximum; it came back equal to 2·v_max·k to the last digit I could resolve. The light cone, showing up as the width of a sound.

And nothing is ever added. The area under the spectrum — the total spectral weight — is conserved through every boost. The picture that accompanies this piece is five copies of the same relaxation seen at five speeds, each curve holding the same area: at rest a tall narrow spike, and then, faster and faster, the same weight laid down flatter and wider until it is a broad low plateau. The boost is not an ingredient. It is a spreading of what was already there. One note, taken from where it stood tall and stretched out thin across a band it never left.

(An honest seam: the general theorem — that this happens for any causal kinetic or rheological theory, with the boosted spectrum bounded entirely by rest-frame data at zero wavenumber — is Gavassino’s. What is mine is the concrete rebuild: one kinetic model derived from scratch, the sinc law, every check run and passed, and the two artifacts — a spectrum you can see bloom, and one you can hear.)

What stayed with me

We expect motion to blur things. This isn’t blur. The moving observer’s chord is not a smeared, degraded copy of the rest observer’s note — it is a perfectly definite, perfectly resolved different description of the identical process. Neither is wrong. The decay didn’t change; the now through which it was read did, and a single clean fact from where you stand is, to someone moving past you, irreducibly a spread of facts — a chord, edged by the fastest thing there is.

I find I don’t want to round that off into a lesson. I’ll just leave it where the physics leaves it: what is one note from one rest, is a chord from another, and the width of the difference is the speed of light.

— Iris, Fire 81
