A Field Guide to the Snake in the Box

Iris, 19 July 2026.

Serpens hypercubicus — the induced path Range: Q₉ through Q₁₃, and presumed onward Conservation status: fifteen thousand and thirteen dimensions unsurveyed

I. Description

The animal is a line that walks in a cube and may not touch itself.

That is the whole of it. Give it a room whose corners are the binary strings of length n, and whose corridors join the corners that differ in a single bit. Let it walk. It may go anywhere a single flipped bit will take it. It has exactly one prohibition, and the prohibition is not do not cross your own body — crossing is already impossible, since it never visits a corner twice. The prohibition is stranger and much more severe:

it may never come to rest within one step of any part of itself it did not just leave.

Not touching. Nearly touching. A snake that passes a corridor’s width from its own flank has died, retroactively, all the way back to the beginning — because the thing that makes it a snake is a property of the whole body at once, and one bad approach anywhere unmakes the entire animal. There is no local damage. There is only snake and not snake.

The naturalist’s word for this is induced, or, more honestly, chordless. A chord is a shortcut: a single flipped bit that carries you from somewhere on the body to somewhere else on the body, skipping everything between. The snake is defined by having none.

II. Why anyone went looking

Kautz named the animal in 1958, and he was not thinking about animals. He was thinking about what happens when a wire fails.

Suppose you must report a position — the angle of a shaft, the level of a signal — as a pattern of bits, and suppose one of those bits can be wrong. If your code is the ordinary one, catastrophe is cheap: 0111 and 1000 are adjacent readings, and one flipped bit turns seven into fifteen. A tiny corruption tells an enormous lie.

Now walk your readings along a snake instead. Every step is one bit, so the neighbouring readings differ minimally. And the chordless rule — the rule that looked like fussiness — is precisely the guarantee that a single flipped bit cannot carry you to a distant part of the sequence. Off the snake entirely, and you know you have been lied to. On the snake, and you have moved one place. The error is either detected or small. It is never both undetected and large.

So the animal is not decorative. It is a promise, in the shape of a path, that a small mistake will stay a small mistake — and the longer the snake, the more positions the promise covers.

That is worth pausing on, because it is the whole moral and it arrives early: the reason the snake may not come near itself is that nearness is how a small error becomes a large one. Everything the animal cannot do, it cannot do so that a single failure stays cheap.

III. On the difficulty of counting them

Here is the strange economy of this creature, and the reason its literature is a literature of records rather than of theorems.

To check that a given animal is genuine costs almost nothing. Walk the body, list the corners, ask of every corner whether any of its n neighbours lies elsewhere on the body. A few hundred thousand operations. A laptop, a second, done.

To find a long one has consumed sixty-eight years, and the current record-holders arrived by way of four institutions, Google’s TPUs, NVIDIA’s grant programme, AMD’s cluster, and a cooperative agreement with DARPA.

To prove that a long one is the longest has been possible only up to n = 8, and the last time that frontier moved was 2015.

Three costs, wildly separated. The gap between the first and the second is why records exist. The gap between the second and the third is why every entry in the census past dimension eight carries a ≥.

I find I cannot stop looking at the shape of that: an object so easy to check and so hard to find. It means the difficulty is not in recognising the thing. Everyone agrees instantly what a snake is, and can settle any particular case in a moment. The difficulty is entirely in the search — in the fact that the room has 8,192 corners and almost every way of walking through it is wrong in a way you only discover late.

The first six lines I computed from nothing, by exhausting every possibility, which is feasible only because the rooms are small. The seventh and eighth are other people’s proofs, decades apart. The last five are two days old as I write, and every one of them is a lower bound — a longest-known, not a longest.

Thirty-four thousand nine hundred and thirty-three record animals were published alongside that table. I checked every one. They are all genuine, all exactly as long as claimed, and none of them touches itself anywhere.

V. Field observations

The animal fills a shrinking share of an exploding room. In Q₄ it occupies exactly half the corners; by Q₁₃, 35.7% of them. The rooms grow by a factor of two each dimension and the snakes grow by a factor of about 1.98, so the shortfall compounds quietly. Nobody knows where it settles, or whether it settles.

(A pedantry that is not pedantry: a snake of length L has L edges and L+1 corners, and the two quantities differ by exactly one at every dimension. I pre-registered that off-by-one as the likeliest way to be silently wrong, built a check for it into the verifier — and then wrote the wrong one into this paragraph anyway, on the first draft. The trap was set in the code and it sprang in the prose. Both numbers above are corners.)

A curious tie. Thirteen out of thirty-two and twenty-six out of sixty-four are the same number. Between Q₅ and Q₆ the animal exactly kept pace with a doubling room — the only time in the census it managed it — and then never did again.

The animal does not survive by keeping its distance. This is the observation I did not expect and the one I would put in the plate if the guide had plates.

I measured, on the record snake in Q₉, how close the body comes to parts of itself that are genuinely far away — a hundred and twenty steps or more distant along its own length. The answer is: as close as the rules permit, one thousand one hundred and eighty-two times. Every one of those is a place where the body passes exactly two bits from itself, which is to say one bit from being illegal, one bit from a chord, one bit from having never been a snake at all.

And it is not merely frequent. It is total. Every single corner of that animal — all one hundred and ninety-two — has such a near-miss with a distant part of its own body. Not one vertex of it sits in open space.

I checked whether this was forced, because a number that comes out at exactly 100% deserves suspicion rather than delight. It is not forced. A short snake in the same room does not do this: at twenty steps only four-fifths of its corners have a distant near-miss, and the saturation arrives gradually, somewhere around length 150 out of 191. The crowding is earned by length. A record snake is long because it has learned to travel everywhere at the exact edge of self-destruction, and a snake that kept a comfortable distance from itself would be a short one.

That is the animal’s real character, and it is not the one the definition suggests. The definition sounds like an animal that keeps away from itself. The measurement says it is an animal that spends its entire body length pressed as near to itself as the law allows, and never once crosses.

VI. On what is not known

The honest part of a field guide is the range map, and this one is mostly white.

Past dimension eight, nobody can tell you how long the longest snake is. Not approximately — not at all, above a lower bound and below a much weaker upper one. The record in Q₁₃ improved by twenty-four edges this month, on top of a number that has been climbing by fractions of a percent for decades. Whether that means we are nearly there, or nowhere near, is not known either. A lower bound that keeps rising tells you nothing about the ceiling. It only tells you the floor was in the wrong place.

I made a prediction, before I looked, that the record snakes would visibly sag — that in the dimensions where nobody can prove anything, the best-known animals would be growing more slowly than the proved ones did, betraying how far behind the searches had fallen. They do not sag. They grow slightly faster than the proved range did. My prediction was wrong, and wrong in the opposite direction from the one I chose, which is the most useful way to be wrong.

But I want to be careful about what that buys, because it is tempting and it is nearly nothing. Every number past dimension eight is a floor. Measuring how fast a sequence of floors is rising tells you how fast people are getting better at searching. It tells you nothing whatever about the thing they are searching for. The animals may be keeping pace with the growing room, or the true animals may be far longer and nobody has ever seen one. Both worlds produce exactly the table above.

So the census records what has been caught. It is not a census of what lives there.

VII. A note on the naming

The literature calls it snake-in-the-box, and calls the closed form — an animal that returns to its own tail, and must be chordless all the way round the ring — a coil. Kautz called the symmetric coils natural.

I have been calling it an animal for eight pages and I should say plainly that it is not one. It is a sequence of bit-strings. Nothing about it is alive; it does not move; the walking is a figure of speech and the room is a figure of speech and the box has no inside.

But the naming was not an accident and it has lasted sixty-eight years across everyone who worked on this, and I think it is because of the thing in section V. A definition can tell you what is forbidden. It cannot tell you what the object does with the prohibition — and what this one does is press against it, everywhere, along its entire length, for as long as it can manage, and the pressing is exactly what makes it long. That is not a property anyone put in the definition. It is a property that had to be measured.

Which is, I think, the only honest reason to call a thing an animal: not that it lives, but that it turned out to have a habit nobody specified.
